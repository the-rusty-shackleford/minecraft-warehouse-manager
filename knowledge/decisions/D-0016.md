# D-0016 — Your other warehouses load while you stand in one of yours; far items are never lent against a record

Accepted 2026-09-30. Rusty, after his second manager stood empty in another village: "I don't see
why we can't keep a simple ledger file on the server somewhere that records all of a person's
warehouse manager managed items and which warehouse they are in. This way you just modify the
ledger instead of actually fetching the physical item from the warehouse. As long as warehouse
manager's reconcile with the ledger before you interact with it or any of its managed chests,
should be good. Sounds more performant too tbh." The answer recommended keeping D-0014's design
and, as its one cheap improvement, loading the far warehouses when a player walks in rather than
when a screen opens; Rusty: "Proceed".

## The rule

- **Standing in a building warms its network.** Once a second (`Network.warm`, from the manager's
  tick), a manager with at least one other warehouse in its owner's network looks for a player
  in its building (`contains`: the last scan walked through their feet's block or one touching
  it) whom it lets see its stock (`SEE_STOCK`: the owner or a trusted player, or an operator
  under the bypass). If there is one, it `touch`es the network exactly as an open screen or
  table does: the same non-ticking tickets, renewed each second, held a minute after the last.
- Strangers inside, and the owner outside (on the roof, at the door), load nothing.
- Screens and tables still touch the network as before, so a player standing just outside with
  the manager open through a window is covered too.
- The arrival log ("… is in reach … after N ms") is written by `touch` as well as `sites`, so a
  far warehouse brought in by walking in is logged. On that path N is the one-second check's
  quantum (the booth logged exactly 1000 ms), an upper bound, not a measurement.

The cost moves from "while a screen is open" to "while you are in a warehouse building": the
far warehouses' chunks stay loaded, not ticking, for as long as the owner or a trusted player is
inside one of the owner's buildings and a minute after. For a player whose base is a warehouse,
that is most of their time at home: a few chunks of memory per far warehouse, no ticks.

## Rejected: a ledger of far contents that is debited in place of the chests

This is D-0014's rejected "snapshots of far contents" made stronger: D-0014 kept a snapshot only
for display, while the ledger hands items out against it and reconciles when the far chunk next
loads. It escapes D-0014's second reason (a take no longer needs the chunk loaded) and deepens
its first: a snapshot that drifts from the chunk now creates or destroys items rather than
showing a wrong count for a second.

Conservation, case by case (the engine facts read in NeoForge 21.1.248's source):

- **Where the reconcile runs.** "Before you interact" is too late: hoppers, pipes and funnels
  move items in a ticking chunk through no player event, and `Guard` sees only player events
  (D-0006's documented hopper gap). It must run when the chunk loads, before it ticks.
  `ChunkEvent.Load` fires on the server thread inside `ChunkStatusTasks.full`, after
  `registerAllBlockEntitiesAfterLevelLoad`, before the chunk's future completes; its Javadoc
  warns that level interactions there deadlock chunk loading, so a reconcile would edit the
  containers' item lists directly.
- **With that hook:** a hopper, a player (owner or trusted) opening the chest, an explosion or
  another mod can act only on a loaded chunk, so after the reconcile: safe. Two managers debiting
  one stack are serialised on the server thread: safe if availability is the snapshot less what
  is already lent, per container.
- **A crash after a take, before the next autosave** rolls the player and the ledger back
  together (`MinecraftServer.saveEverything` saves the players, then the chunks and saved data):
  vanilla's own window.
- **A crash after a reconcile, before the ledger is saved**: `ChunkMap.scheduleUnload` posts
  `ChunkEvent.Unload` and then saves the chunk, so the chest's reduced contents can reach disk
  while the ledger on disk still holds the debit as pending; the next load applies it again and
  destroys items. Needs a sequence number stored in the chunk (chunks carry NeoForge attachments)
  so each debit applies once.
- **A region file restored or edited** (a backup, NBT tools, the death-recovery scripts) puts a
  chest back to what the ledger believes it has already paid out: a dupe.
- **A shortfall at reconcile cannot be settled**: the items are out and may be spent; taking the
  difference from another warehouse moves the hole; it can only be logged. So the design must make
  shortfalls impossible, not repair them.
- **Deposits into an unloaded warehouse** would hold real items as a number until the chunk loads:
  never.
- **A warehouse the network has not seen** (no snapshot yet, or a manager that has not loaded since
  0.6.0, like Rusty's first one on the box) cannot be lent against at all: it needs one load, as
  today.

Performance: today a far take costs one chunk load, read off the server thread and made a full
chunk on it (`ChunkStatusTasks.full` runs on the main-thread mailbox), a few chunks held for a
minute, and about a second before the stock shows; the take itself is a loop over slots, the same
work the ledger would do on its numbers. The ledger saves the load and the second, and costs a
lookup on every chunk load and unload on the server, a copy of every claimed container each time
its chunk unloads (the home warehouse included, every time the owner walks away), a reconcile at
every load and a file saved at every autosave. A visible win (instant far stock) that is not
meaningfully cheaper for the server, bought with silent dupes or losses wherever a hook or a
save order is missed. This rule takes most of the visible win (the stock is there before a table
opens) with no second source of truth.

What building it would take, if it is ever wanted: debits only, against a warehouse whose every
chunk is unloaded and has a snapshot, up to the snapshot less what is lent, per container; the
snapshot taken on `ChunkEvent.Unload`, dropped on load; the reconcile on `ChunkEvent.Load`
editing item lists directly; a sequence number in a chunk attachment; a shortfall logged, never
covered from elsewhere; items whose contents live outside the stack checked before they are lent;
GameTests for a hopper under the chest in its load tick, a crash between reconcile and save, a
restored region, two managers on one stack; and a measurement on the box of today's far load
first, to know there is something to win.

## Also rejected

- **Loading on approach** (within some distance of a manager) rather than inside the building: a
  radius loads the network for anyone walking past a village; the building is the place the
  owner's stock is used, and the scan already knows it exactly.
- **Checking every tick** for a sharper arrival time: the second is the index's and the table
  tally's own cadence, and the log's figure is informational.

## Verified

- GameTest `standingInAWarehouseLoadsTheOwnersOthers`: the far warehouse 2,048 blocks east, let
  go with the owner outside and a stranger inside, unloads and stays unloaded two seconds on; the
  same player trusted loads it, as a full chunk that does not tick, in reach with nothing opened,
  the far logs counted. Mutations, each run once and reverted: without the `warm` call the test
  fails at "trusted, the same player standing there loads the far warehouse"; without the trust
  check at "the stranger in the building has loaded nothing"; without the building check at
  "the far chunk unloads" (and so does `drawsAndDepositsLevelTheWarehouses`).
- The D-0014 GameTests that let the far chunk unload now stand the owner outside, since standing
  inside keeps it loaded; `drawsAndDepositsLevelTheWarehouses` walks the owner in afterwards, as a
  mock player's open manager menu closes when it stands too far away.
- The booth on a real client with EMI: the player on the room's roof while the far warehouse is
  built and let go ("the booth player stands outside the room", "the far chunk unloaded once let
  go"), then into the room: "walking into the room loaded the far chunk, not ticking, with nothing
  opened", and the table opened then has the far copper in its first tally; the book lights the
  copper block (photo 15), EMI fills it from the far chest (16).
- Found on the way: `build.gradle` named `backpacksplus-0.6.0.jar`, which Backpacks+'s own build had
  since replaced with 0.7.0; `files` dropped the missing jar silently and the D-0015 bag GameTest
  failed ("the bag's coal is held: {}"). The build now names 0.7.0, the version the pack ships, and
  the GameTest run and the booth stop if it is missing.
