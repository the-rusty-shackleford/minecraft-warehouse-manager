# D-0012 — A part the building could make counts, and the fill makes it

Accepted 2026-09-25. Rusty, on pack 1.63.0: a rifle whose receivers are themselves craftable from
what the building holds should show as craftable, and the fill should be able to make the
sub-parts. Their three calls, asked before the design: **the full tree** (not one level); **crafting-
table recipes only** (no furnace); **the fill makes the sub-parts** (not a filter that lights the
rifle and a click that says "short of 1 × Lower Receiver").

The real tree, read off the pack's jars: rifle ← upper receiver (4 steel) + lower receiver
(3 steel + redstone) + barrel (3 iron) + stock (3 planks + stick); Metals and Materials makes
steel at the table (3 iron + coal → 3) and has the ingot ↔ nugget ↔ block cycle. From iron, coal,
logs and redstone the rifle is four levels deep.

## The rule

`domain.Expansion`, pure. A recipe is a list of cells, one per non-empty pattern cell, each the
items it accepts. `plan(cells, crafts, available, rules, depth)` walks the cells in the recipe's
order; a cell takes the first option of which `crafts` are on hand, else the first that can be
made up to that number (**one kind per cell for all the crafts of a click**, the constraint
vanilla's own placement has: `RecipePicker.tryPick` takes `amount` of one item per ingredient);
an item is made by the first of its rules whose cells can in turn be covered, one level shallower,
as many times as its yield needs, never while the same item is being made higher up (the cycle
guard); a rule that fails is backed out before the next is tried; what a step makes beyond the
need stays available to later cells; a cell that fails is reported short by what its best option
cannot cover and the walk goes on so every shortage is named. Steps come out leaves first and
runs of the same rule with the same picks fold into one. The rule book orders an item's rules by
**yield ascending, then id**: three steel come from iron and coal (three a craft) before a block
is broken into nine. `most` bisects for the largest covered count, as vanilla's `tryPickAll`;
`reachable` is the count-free fixpoint both books check before a plan and what makes the rifle a
candidate in EMI at all. Depth is a constant, 8.

The choice is greedy and never revisited, so a plan that is found is exact and executable, and a
plan that is not found may exist (a later alternative would have left more for an earlier cell).
Documented as such; vanilla's placement has the same greedy shape. Shortages are reported by the
same walk with no rules, so D-0008's message is unchanged.

## Where it lives

- Server, `Pooled.pull`: one craft planned first (not covered: log, chat, return, as D-0008);
  the stack cap from that plan's picks, `most` from the expansion, vanilla's `wanted` rule
  (D-0010) over it, the steps for `min(wanted, most)` crafts. Each step: the picks' shortfall is
  drawn from the chests, then per craft one of each pick is taken out of the inventory (or the
  grid, which vanilla is about to clear into the inventory anyway; the grid is not cleared first,
  which would break D-0010's piling), laid out in the rule's pattern, checked with the recipe's own
  `matches`, assembled, the crafting stat, the crafting event, the recipe trigger and the recipe
  award applied as `ResultSlot` does, the result and remainders given (dropped at the feet only
  when nothing fits). Then the path of 0.4.1 unchanged: vanilla's craftability, `Pooling.pull`,
  the draw. One INFO line per step and a gray chat line per fill naming what was made.
- The rule book, `Pooled.rules`: shaped and shapeless recipes (a mod's own recipe class can be
  clicked, not made as a part, since only those two are laid out and matched here), not special,
  every ingredient simple with an item, a result with no components; cached per recipe set
  (rebuilt on a reload or a join), 889 rules from 903 crafting recipes in 19 ms on the test
  server. Under `doLimitedCrafting` reduced to the player's unlocked recipes (D-0007).
- Vanilla book: `Craftables` begins a pass at the head of `RecipeBookComponent.updateCollections`
  over the component's tally when the open table is managed; `RecipeCollectionMixin` at the tail of
  `RecipeCollection.canCraft` adds to the craftable set the recipes vanilla found fitting and known
  that the pass covers. Shown as plain craftable; no distinct marking.
- EMI: the handler's inventory over a managed table is `Expanded`, a subclass of
  `EmiPlayerInventory` (public, virtual): `canCraft` is EMI's answer or the plan's;
  `getCraftAvailability` marks no input missing when the plan covers the recipe; `getCraftables`
  gathers candidates by the inputs on hand **and** by the reachable items, since EMI itself only
  looks at inputs on hand, then applies EMI's own filter and order (`EmiFavorite.Craftable`,
  `EmiStackList`: internals, the same standing as the handler list of D-0005, asserted by the
  booth). Elsewhere the plain inventory.

## Rejected

- Virtual counts (adding what could be made to the tally as if it were there): lights recipes
  the same materials cannot cover twice (4 planks read as 8 sticks and a crafting table at once).
- Instant smelting as a step: skips furnace time and fuel.
- Furnace recipes in the filter with the fill stopping at them: the book would light what a click
  cannot finish, the confusion D-0008 removed.
- Clearing the grid before the steps: breaks D-0010's piling, whose count vanilla reads off the
  grid after the mixin returns.

## Verified

JUnit `ExpansionTest` (16, partitions at the top). GameTests: a torch with a log and a coal in the
chest makes planks and sticks, fills, leaves the surplus with the player, unlocks the two recipes
and counts the stats; a shift-click makes parts for three crafts; under `doLimitedCrafting` a
locked planks recipe is not used until unlocked. Booth with EMI: the vanilla book lights the torch
and the planks with only logs and coal in the room, EMI's inventory is the expanded one, lists the
torch, shows no input missing, fills it; with the pack's two gun jars the rifle from iron, coal,
redstone, planks and a stick, the four made parts in the grid. Photos in
`devtools/verification/0.5.0/`.
