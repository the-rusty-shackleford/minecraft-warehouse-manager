# D-0014 — One owner's warehouses are one network

Accepted 2026-09-27. Rusty, after buying several villages far apart: "sharing my inventory across
such large spaces became incredibly cumbersome. I was thinking that linking warehouse managers up
together such that placing another warehouse manager in another village pools both inventories
together. It doesn't even matter much which physical chests something lives in (although
splitting them evenly where possible would be a good redundancy) since you can pull from all of
them from any warehouse manager you own."

Asked where a deposit should go (balanced, here with spill-over, or here only), Rusty chose
**balanced**. The rest was decided in the plan he approved.

## The rule

- **A network is everything one player owns**, found by ownership alone: placing (or claiming) a
  manager makes it a member of its owner's network, in any dimension. An unowned manager is a
  network of one. One building, one manager (D-0006) stands.
- **Everything that read one building reads the network in reach**: the index and its takes
  (D-0009), the recipe book's and EMI's tally and the fill with its made parts (D-0004, D-0012).
  The warehouse at hand comes first, then the others nearest first.
- **Balanced placement** (`domain.Spread`). A stack leaving the manager's buffer goes to the
  warehouse in reach that holds the least of its kind and has a container for its group, then the
  next; a tie stays here, then goes to the nearest. A draw (index take, table fill, parts) levels
  from the top: one at a time from whichever holds the most, so the warehouses end as even as the
  draw allows. Nothing already stored moves between warehouses; each warehouse's own sort pass is
  unchanged. Over time every kind splits about evenly between the villages, which is the
  redundancy Rusty wanted.
- **One roster per owner**, as Village Deed's D-0004 did for villages on Rusty's "apply to all
  of my villages". With pooling a per-manager roster would be incoherent: trusted at A would mean
  drawing from B through A's screen. A toggle at any manager's panel applies at all of them, and
  the panel's hint says how many it covers.

## Where it lives

- `Network`, saved data `warehousemanager_network` on the **overworld**: per owner, the roster
  (id and last name) and the members, each a manager position (with dimension), its containers
  nearest the manager first, and its plan's routes (which containers take each leaf). A member
  is recorded at its every scan and on claim, and removed when its block is broken, or found gone
  in a loaded chunk. The containers and routes are stored because a far manager, loaded without
  ticking, never scans.
- `Network.sites(manager)` is the network as reachable **now**, and loads nothing: a member is in
  reach when every chunk of its manager and containers is loaded. The check is `getChunkNow`, not
  `hasChunkAt`: in 21.1.248 `hasChunk` answers true as soon as a ticket asks for the chunk, and a
  block read then blocks the server thread until the load finishes (read in `ServerChunkCache`).
- `Network.touch(manager)` adds a ticket per member chunk: `TicketType` `warehousemanager_network`,
  `addRegionTicket(type, chunk, 0, chunk)`, level 33, a full chunk (block entities readable and
  writable) that does not tick blocks or entities; lifespan 1,200 ticks, renewed by adding it
  again (which resets its age). It is called when a manager's screen or a table in a managed
  building opens, and once a second while either stays open. Nothing ever loads a chunk
  synchronously: what is not in reach yet is simply not counted, and arrives within about a
  second (801 ms measured in the booth, 6 to 80 ms in the GameTest world). The table's tally is
  sent again when the set of warehouses in reach changes (`Pooled.refresh`); the vanilla book and
  EMI rebuild on it.
- The block entity keeps only its owner. `ownership()` composes the owner with the network's
  cached `Access.Ownership` for them, so `Access`, `Guard` and every `permits` call are unchanged.
- Counting uses a hash map keyed by item and components (`ItemStackLinkedSet.TYPE_AND_TAG`),
  replacing the index's linear scan over kinds, which a network would multiply.
- The wire changed (`Index.Row` carries a per-warehouse breakdown for the tooltip, the roster
  listing how many warehouses it covers): registrar `"2"` → `"3"`.

## The file (0.5.x per-manager rosters)

A manager's own `Trusted` list, saved on its block before 0.6.0, is folded into its owner's roster
when the block loads (`onLoad`): everyone on it joins the owner's list, nobody loses access they
had anywhere. The fold happens **once per manager**, recorded in the network data (`Folded`),
because the block keeps its old copy until its chunk next saves it: without the record, a player
the owner withdrew could be trusted again by the stale copy loading. The block no longer saves a
roster. On the box (read from `warehousemanager_claims.dat` 2026-09-27) there is one manager,
Jdrum12's at -1570, 68, -355, so the fold concerns one list.

## Cost

A far warehouse costs nothing while nobody uses the network. In use, its chunks are held as full,
non-ticking chunks (a full ticket also reads the chunks around it at low statuses, as a
`/forceload` does); villagers, farms and redstone there do not run. One log line when a member is
asked for ("loading the warehouse at … : N chunk(s)") and one when it arrives ("… is in reach …
after N ms"), so the cost is measured, not assumed. The deposit path looks the network up once a
second and counts each warehouse's kinds once per second at most, updating the counts in place
per move; the idle tick is unchanged.

## Rejected

- **Snapshots of far contents** (counted at unload, exact while unloaded since nothing changes an
  unloaded chunk): instant display, but a second source of truth to keep in step with the chunk
  across crashes and saves, and every take or fill from a far warehouse still needs it loaded.
- **Loading far chunks synchronously** on a take or fill: a hitch for everyone on the server.
- **Letting the far manager tick** (block-ticking tickets) so it drains a remote buffer: its
  flood fill treats unloaded cells as walls, and a building only partly loaded would scan small
  and release claims on the rest.
- **Per-manager rosters with both needed**, or a network roster beside them: two lists to reason
  about for a case nobody raised.
- **Splitting one stack between warehouses on deposit**: stack by stack to the scarcest already
  levels over time, and a draw levels exactly.
- **Explicit linking** (an item or an action): Rusty described linking by placing a manager.

## Verified

- JUnit `SpreadTest`: deposit order (none, one, several; ties by here, distance, id; closed
  warehouses), draws (under, equal to and over what is held; equal holdings; the remainder by the
  tie order), a 2,000-case comparison with taking one at a time, and bad inputs.
- Four GameTests on a real server, the far warehouse built 2,048 blocks east of the test under a
  temporary forced chunk, scanned, then let go until `getChunkNow` is null: unloaded it counts
  nothing and counting loads nothing; the network's ticket brings it back full and not ticking,
  and the index counts it with the breakdown; a right-click take levels forty cobblestone (thirty
  far, ten near) to ten and ten; a near table's planks draw the log only the far chest holds; sand
  deposits go far (none there, twenty here), cobblestone stays here on a tie; one roster covers
  both managers (trust at the far one, withdraw at the near one's panel); broken managers leave;
  a 0.5.x roster folds once and is not re-trusted by the stale copy.
- The booth on a real client with EMI: a second warehouse 2,048 blocks east, unloaded; a table in
  the room first counts the room alone, then a second tally (801 ms later) counts the far copper,
  the vanilla book and EMI light the copper block, EMI's fill takes the nine ingots from the far
  chest; the index tooltip reads "104 in your warehouses: 64 here, 40 at 2052, 4" (photos 15–17).
  The "room alone" check is made on the server in the tick the table opens: made on the client a
  few ticks later it raced a far chunk whose data was cached (48 ms) and the second tally.
