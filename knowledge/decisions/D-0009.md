# D-0009 — The manager's screen is the warehouse's index

Accepted 2026-09-24. Rusty: "its really easy to put things into the warehouse via the manager,
but its quite hard to locate things/just take inventory of what you have. … the existing
warehouse manager section in the UI lists all of the warehouse chest contents in a deterministic
order, and exposes a search bar that filters the shown items accordingly. In between that section
and the inventory section is a single box labeled Insert, which is where you put things from
your inventory and then it disappears into the manager … You can also take things out directly
from the warehouse manager section into your inventory."

Rusty's three calls, asked and answered before the design was written:

- **Order: by group, then name.** Grouped the way the chests are labelled, a heading per group,
  items alphabetical inside; the search collapses it to the matches.
- **Taking: like a chest slot.** Left lifts a stack onto the cursor, right half; a loaded cursor
  puts its stack into the warehouse (all on left, one on right, shift or not); shift-click with an
  empty cursor sends a stack straight to the inventory.
- **Search matches name, group and mod** (`@create` for a mod, as EMI does).

## What the screen is now

A grid of nine columns and five rows of 18 px cells with a scrollbar, inside the manager's own
screen (194 wide, 224 tall, the trust panel of D-0006 beside it unchanged). Each kind of item the
building holds is one cell: its icon, its total across every chest and the buffer at half size in
the corner (1.5K past 999), a tooltip with the item's own lines, "N in the warehouse" and its
heading. Headings are the taxonomy's leaf under its parent, in the taxonomy's order ("Building /
Stone" … "Misc"), the same words the signs use; the server sends the order with the listing so
the client needs no taxonomy. A search box sits in the title row. Under the grid, one **Insert**
slot; under that, the inventory.

The six-row chest is gone from the screen but not from the block: the manager's buffer is still
where things go before the drain routes them, and it is what the Insert slot sinks into, at once,
on the server; a stack the buffer has no room for stays in the slot where the player can see it,
and comes back to the inventory when the screen closes. Shift-click from the inventory goes
straight into the buffer. Furnishing (D-0003) still reads the buffer, so chests dropped into the
Insert slot still stand themselves along the walls.

Taking is a packet naming the kind (a stack of one, item and components) and the click; the
server decides with the domain's `Take.decide`, pulls from the chests nearest first and then the
buffer with `ManagerBlockEntity.pull`, and puts the result on the cursor or in the inventory;
vanilla syncs the cursor. Owner and trusted may take (`DRAW`); whoever may open the screen may
insert. The listing is re-sent every second to everyone with the manager open, so a chest
emptied by hand shows within a second, and at once after every click.

## Rejected

- **A trie over the contents** (Rusty: "it might be nice … but you decide"). A trie answers
  prefix queries; the search matches words anywhere in the name, the group or the mod, which a
  trie cannot do without a suffix structure, and the index is a few hundred kinds at most, so a
  linear pass per keystroke is microseconds. The tree the contents live in is the taxonomy.
- Keeping the six-row chest beside the index: two ways in, one of them invisible once the index
  exists; the Insert slot is the one way and the index shows where things went.
- Cursor semantics predicted on the client: the server owns the chests, and a wrong prediction
  would show a stack that does not exist; one round trip on a LAN or a local server is invisible.
- A separate "index" block or a command: the manager is where players already go.

Verified by JUnit over `Index` (order, matching, rows) and `Take` (every button, cursor and count
partition), three GameTests (the listing's kinds, totals and headings; the takes for an unowned
manager, a stranger and a trusted player; the Insert slot sinking, draining, refusing a full
buffer and handing back on close), and the booth: the index photographed, the search for "iron"
showing the ingot and the pickaxe, a stack of cobblestone lifted onto the cursor and gone from
the index, then put back through the Insert slot and counted again.
